     1→# The Kenya Abduction Monitoring & Prediction System (KAMPS): Final Implementation Plan
     2→
     3→## Executive Summary
     4→
     5→**KAMPS is a modular early-warning system built on verified data, cross-validated sources, and ethical methodological standards that produces zone-level risk assessments and identifies suspected abduction vehicles through pattern recognition.** The system addresses Kenya's documented pattern of enforced disappearances — 423 cases recorded by Missing Voices since 2019, 125 police killings and 6 disappearances in 2025 alone, and 69 killings and 19 disappearances in the first eight months of 2026.【turn0search8】【turn0search12】【turn0search14】
     6→
     7→The plan that follows represents the integration of everything discussed: aggregate risk scoring corrected for population and reporting bias, vehicle re-identification for pattern detection, secure data infrastructure, and rigorous verification following the Berkeley Protocol.

   129→### 2.2 Entity Resolution (Deduplication)
   130→
   131→The same incident will appear in multiple sources with variations. **Splink** is the solution.
   132→
   133→**Splink** is a Python package for probabilistic record linkage (entity resolution) developed by the UK Ministry of Justice Analytical Services. It deduplicates and links records from datasets that lack unique identifiers.【turn1search0】【turn1search1】【turn1search2】
   134→
   135→**Key capabilities:**
   136→- Links a million records on a laptop in approximately one minute
   137→- Term frequency adjustments and fuzzy matching logic
   138→- Unsupervised learning — no training data required
   139→- Interactive outputs for reviewing match decisions
   140→- Scales to Spark for 100+ million records
   141→
   142→**Implementation for KAMPS:**
   143→
   144→```python
   145→from splink import splink_datasets
   146→import splink.comparison_library as cl
   147→
   148→# Define comparison for linking incident records
   149→settings = {
   150→    "link_type": "dedupe_only",
   151→    "comparisons": [
   152→        cl.exact_match("victim_name"),
   153→        cl.jaro_winkler_at_thresholds("victim_name", 0.8),
   154→        cl.date_of_birth_comparison("incident_date"),
   155→        cl.geographic_distance_comparison(
   156→            "latitude", "longitude", 
   157→            km_thresholds=[1, 5, 10]
   158→        ),
   159→        cl.exact_match("source_type"),
   160→    ],
   161→    "blocking_rules_to_generate_predictions": [
   162→        "l.victim_name_first_letter = r.victim_name_first_letter",
   163→        "l.incident_month = r.incident_month",
   164→    ]
   165→}
   166→
   167→# Run linkage
   168→linker = Splink(df_incidents, settings)
   169→results = linker.predict()
   170→```

   177→### 2.3 Verification Framework (Berkeley Protocol Standard)
   178→
   179→The **Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations** sets international standards for conducting online research of alleged human rights violations. It requires:【turn0search3】【turn0search4】
   180→
   181→1. **Chain of custody preservation** — every data point must have a documented provenance
   182→2. **Verification before analysis** — no unverified data enters the analytical pipeline
   183→3. **Protection of sources** — the safety of witnesses, victims, and first responders is paramount
   184→4. **Professional methodology** — investigations must meet standards suitable for legal proceedings
   185→
   186→**Verification checklist for each incident:**
   187→
   188→```
   189→□ Source reliability assessed (Tier 1/2/3)
   190→□ At least two independent sources confirm key details
   191→□ Geolocation verified (if location-based claim)
   192→□ Temporal verification (date/time cross-checked)
   193→□ Content authentication (if media: EXIF, reverse image search, 
   194→  frame analysis for AI-generation)
   195→□ Witness/source protection assessed
   196→□ Documentation of verification decision and rationale
   197→```

   219→## Part 3: Risk Assessment Engine (Corrected for Bias)
   220→
   221→### 3.1 The Multi-Stage Analytical Pipeline
   222→
   223→```
   224→Stage 1: Rate Calculation
   225→├── Crude rate = incidents / population × 100,000
   226→├── Using HRSL 30m gridded population data
   227→└── Output: Raw per-capita rates by sub-location
   228→
   229→Stage 2: Empirical Bayes Smoothing
   230→├── Poisson-Gamma model: shrink small-area rates toward global mean
   231→├── Prevents spurious high rates from low-population areas
   232→└── Output: Stabilized rate estimates
   233→
   234→Stage 3: Multiple Systems Estimation (MSE)
   235→├── Estimate underreporting by zone using capture-recapture
   236→├── Compare overlap between Missing Voices, police OB, 
   237→│   hospital records, news reports, court records
   238→└── Output: Underreporting adjustment factors by zone
   239→
   240→Stage 4: Spatial Scan Statistics
   241→├── SaTScan: detect statistically significant clusters
   242→├── Adjusts for population, tests against random distribution
   243→└── Output: Significant clusters with relative risk ratios
   244→
   245→Stage 5: Temporal Anomaly Detection
   246→├── Rolling 90-day baseline per zone
   247→├── Flag zones with >2 standard deviations from baseline
   248→└── Output: Temporal alert flags
   249→
   250→Stage 6: Composite Risk Index
   251→├── Weighted aggregation of all indicators
   252→├── Weights determined by expert judgment + sensitivity analysis
   253→└── Output: Final zone-level risk score (0-100)
   254→```
   255→
   256→### 3.2 The SaTScan Implementation
   257→
   258→SaTScan is free software that analyzes spatial, temporal, and space-time data using spatial scan statistics.【turn0search17】【turn0search18】【turn0search19】
   259→
   260→**Configuration for Kenya abductions:**
   261→
   262→```
   263→Probability model: Poisson (count data)
   264→Scan for: High rates only
   265→Spatial window: Variable size, up to 50% of population at risk
   266→Time adjustment: Yes (adjust for temporal trends)
   267→Monte Carlo: 999 replications for p-value estimation
   268→Population file: HRSL-derived population by sub-location
   269→Case file: Verified incidents with coordinates and dates
   270→Coordinates: Sub-location centroids (or incident coordinates when known)
   271→
   272→Output:
   273→- Primary cluster(s): geographic areas with significantly 
   274→  elevated risk (p < 0.05)
   275→- Relative risk: "Risk inside cluster is X× higher than outside"
   276→- Log likelihood ratio: strength of evidence
   277→```
   278→
   279→**What SaTScan does NOT do:** It does not simply flag "where most incidents occur" — it identifies areas where incidents are **significantly more common than expected** given the population at risk. This solves the "Nairobi always wins" problem.
   280→
   281→### 3.3 MSE for Underreporting
   282→
   283→The **Human Rights Data Analysis Group (HRDAG)** pioneered Multiple Systems Estimation for human rights documentation.【turn0search7】【turn0search8】【turn0search9】
   284→
   285→**Kenya-specific implementation:**
   286→
   287→```
   288→Datasets for MSE:
   289→1. Missing Voices verified incidents
   290→2. Kenya Police OB records (via ATI Act request)
   291→3. KNH/mortuary unclaimed bodies registry
   292→4. Nairobi Funeral Home records
   293→5. News reports (Daily Nation, Standard, The Star)
   294→6. Court records (habeas corpus, inquest files)
   295→7. IPOA complaints database
   296→
   297→For each zone (sub-county):
   298→- Compile case lists from each source
   299→- Deduplicate using Splink
   300→- Model capture probabilities for each source
   301→- Estimate total unobserved cases
   302→
   303→Example result:
   304→- Nairobi: 20 documented, MSE estimates 25 total (factor: 1.25)
   305→- Turkana: 3 documented, MSE estimates 8 total (factor: 2.67)
   306→
   307→Adjusted count = Documented × Underreporting Factor
   308→```
   309→
   310→**Critical assumption:** The datasets must be *independent* — the probability of a case appearing in Missing Voices should not depend on whether it appeared in police OB records. If they are correlated, MSE underestimates the true count.
   311→
   312→### 3.4 Spatial Regression for Excess Risk
   313→
   314→Identify zones with **more incidents than predicted by their characteristics**:
   315→
   316→```python
   317→# Python implementation using PySAL
   318→from pysal.model import spreg
   319→from pysal.lib import weights
   320→import geopandas as gpd
   321→
   322→# Load zones with incident counts and covariates
   323→zones = gpd.read_file("kenya_subcounties_with_data.shp")
   324→
   325→# Spatial weights matrix
   326→w = weights.Queen.from_dataframe(zones)
   327→
   328→# Spatial lag model: incidents ~ population + poverty + urbanicity + W_incidents
   329→model = spreg.ML_Lag(
   330→    zones[['log_population', 'poverty_rate', 'urbanicity']].values,
   331→    zones['incident_count'].values,
   332→    w=w,
   333→    name_y='incident_count',
   334→    name_x=['log_population', 'poverty_rate', 'urbanicity']
   335→)
   336→
   337→# Residuals = excess risk beyond what covariates predict
   338→zones['excess_risk'] = model.u
   339→
   340→# Map zones with significantly positive residuals
   341→```
   342→
   343→**R alternative using `spatialreg`:**
   344→
   345→```r
   346→library(spatialreg)
   347→library(spdep)
   348→
   349→nb <- poly2nb(kenya_zones)
   350→lw <- nb2listw(nb, style = "W")
   351→
   352→model <- lagsarlm(
   353→  incident_count ~ log_population + poverty_rate + urbanicity,
   354→  data = kenya_zones,
   355→  listw = lw
   356→)
   357→
   358→kenya_zones$excess_risk <- residuals(model)
   359→```

   552→## Part 6: Legal & Ethical Framework
   553→
   554→### 6.1 Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 Compliance
   555→
   556→The Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) enforces Kenya's primary data protection statute.【turn0search17】
   557→
   558→**Lawful basis options:**
   559→- **Legitimate interests** (human rights documentation)
   560→- **Public task** (if working with registered CSO)
   561→- **Consent** (from data subjects where possible)
   562→
   563→**Required compliance measures:**
   564→- Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before deployment
   565→- Documented lawful basis for each processing activity
   566→- Data minimization: collect only what is necessary
   567→- Data subject rights procedures (access, rectification, erasure)
   568→- Cross-border transfer assessments if any data leaves Kenya
   569→
   570→### 6.2 Access to Information Act 2016
   571→
   572→Every Kenyan citizen has the right to access information held by the state under Article 35 of the Constitution and the ATI Act 2016.【turn6search0】【turn6search1】【turn6search2】
   573→
   574→**Strategic ATI requests for KAMPS:**
   575→
   576→1. **Police OB records:** Request anonymized abduction/disappearance reports from digitized stations【turn0search11】【turn0search12】【turn0search13】
   577→2. **Hospital mortuary registries:** Request unclaimed bodies data with dates, causes of death, originating police stations
   578→3. **IPOA investigation summaries:** Request aggregate data on complaints received and investigation outcomes
   579→4. **County government disposal records:** Request documentation of body disposals, including which police stations originated the bodies
   580→
   581→### 6.3 Ethical Principles (Berkeley Protocol + OHCHR)
   582→
   583→**OHCHR Human Rights-Based Approach to Data (HRBAD)** espouses six key principles:【turn0search17】
   584→
   585→1. **Participation** — affected communities must be involved in all stages of the data lifecycle
   586→2. **Self-identification** — people must be able to self-identify rather than be externally categorized
   587→3. **Data disaggregation** — data must be disaggregated to reveal inequalities
   588→4. **Privacy** — data collection must protect individual privacy
   589→5. **Transparency** — methodology must be open and documented
   590→6. **Accountability** — data collectors must be accountable to data subjects
   591→
   592→**Do No Harm principles for satellite imagery:**【turn3search14】【turn3search15】
   593→- Never publish imagery that could identify specific individuals or homes without consent
   594→- Consider whether publishing location data could enable retaliation
   595→- Satellite imagery should complement, not replace, ground-truth verification
   596→- Always consider the perspective of people on the ground who may be endangered by publication

   735→## Part 10: Critical Success Factors
   736→
   737→### 10.1 What Makes This System Different
   738→
   739→**Not just a heatmap.** The system produces statistically defensible risk assessments that:
   740→- Adjust for population density (no more "Nairobi always wins")
   741→- Estimate underreporting (MSE reveals hidden rural risk)
   742→- Identify excess risk beyond what demographics predict
   743→- Flag temporal anomalies (zones with sudden risk elevation)
   744→- Document methodology transparently (Berkeley Protocol compliance)
   745→
   746→**Not just data collection.** The system integrates:
   747→- Verified data only (multi-source confirmation required)
   748→- Entity resolution (deduplication prevents double-counting)
   749→- Chain of custody (every data point has documented provenance)
   750→- Source reliability hierarchy (Tier 1/2/3 classification)
   751→
   752→**Not just prediction.** The system provides:
   753→- Actionable intelligence for CSO protective response
   754→- Evidence base for advocacy and legal accountability
   755→- Public awareness of geographic risk patterns
   756→- Documentation suitable for international mechanisms
   757→
   758→### 10.2 What Could Go Wrong (And Mitigations)
   759→
   760→| Risk | Mitigation |
   761→|---|---|
   762→| Database compromise creates kill list | Air-gapped master, aggregate-only outputs, no individual rankings |
   763→| System predicts wrong zone, CSO deploys resources incorrectly | Confidence intervals, multiple indicators, human review before action |
   764→| Adversary learns of system and changes tactics | Regular methodology review, adapt detection thresholds |
   765→| Data subjects harmed by publication | Strict output controls, no individual-identifying data, community consultation before publication |
   766→| Legal challenge under Data Protection Act | DPIA conducted, lawful basis documented, data minimization enforced |
   767→| Partner organization infiltrated | Need-to-know compartmentalization, code names, verification protocols |
   768→
   769→### 10.3 The Philosophical Commitment
   770→
   771→**The system exists to protect people, not to predict them.**
   772→
   773→Every design decision — aggregate outputs, strict verification, community consultation, ethical review — flows from this principle. A system that identifies "who will be kidnapped next" is functionally a targeting list. A system that identifies "which zones have statistically elevated risk after adjusting for population and underreporting" is a protective tool.
   774→
   775→The difference is not semantic. It is the difference between a weapon and a shield.
